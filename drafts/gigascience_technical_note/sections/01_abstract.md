# Abstract

**Background:** Variant annotators repeatedly compute the same consequences
when variants recur across samples. We developed VCFcache to reuse annotations
generated by a fixed annotation recipe and to submit only cache misses to the
original annotator.

**Findings:** We benchmarked end-to-end annotation of 52 held-out whole
genomes using two bundled gnomAD caches and disjoint three-genome cohort
caches. With Ensembl VEP, median speedups were 4.75-fold, 9.21-fold and
4.59-fold for the gnomAD any-stratum allele-frequency thresholds of 10% and
1% and the cohort cache, respectively. The corresponding speedups with the
faster native annotator fastVEP were 1.84-fold, 2.05-fold and 1.81-fold. All
312 cached outputs passed annotator-specific semantic comparison. In a real
4.80-million-variant genome, controlled VEP pipelines spanning 4.2–22.7 hours
were reduced to 19.7–21.7% of direct runtime at 80.23% cache hits and
9.9–11.9% at 90.26% hits.

**Conclusions:** Annotation caching provides substantial savings across fast
and slow pipelines. Relative acceleration is governed mainly by cache hit rate
and fixed lookup overhead, whereas absolute time saved increases with
annotation cost.
